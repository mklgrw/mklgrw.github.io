Privacy Policy

Effective as of September 14, 2026.

California Notice at Collection / State Privacy Rights Notice. See the State privacy rights notice section below for important information about your rights under applicable state privacy laws.

LayerTWO Lab LLC ("LifeMerge," "Company," "we," "us," or "our") provides the LifeMerge iOS application (also referred to historically or internally as GitLife), including related hosted account, sync, and connection features (collectively, the "Service"). The Service lets you view personal activity as contribution-style graphs, including Apple Health daily totals, GitHub contribution counts, and categorized bank spending, after you choose to connect those sources.

This Privacy Policy describes how we process personal information that we collect through the Service. It is adapted from General Legal’s U.S. Privacy Policy template. It does not cover third-party products that you connect (Apple, Plaid, GitHub, and their policies apply to those services).

Operator: The Service is operated by LayerTWO Lab LLC. Apple developer team branding may still show LAYERTWO / related bundle IDs (com.getlife.prototype, com.gitlife.prototype) in App Store Connect and builds.

Personal information we collect

Information you provide to us

Personal information you may provide to us through the Service or otherwise includes:

  1. Account and contact data, such as the name and email address Apple returns when you use Sign in with Apple (Apple may supply a private-relay email), and a display name you later edit in Settings.
  2. Profile data, such as the display name stored on your LifeMerge account.
  3. Communications data based on our exchanges with you if you contact us about the Service.
  4. User corrections and requests, such as a manual category or note you attach to an imported purchase, and optional “add an aspect of life” requests (for example Garmin Connect, WHOOP, Fitbit, Oura, or a custom app name). Those requests do not include Health values or bank amounts.
  5. Other data not specifically listed here, which we will use as described in this Privacy Policy or as otherwise disclosed at the time of collection.

We do not ask you to provide government-issued identification numbers, payment-card numbers to buy the app (the public launch is intended to be free, with no in-app purchase in the current product), billing addresses, photographs, public social-network profiles, or contest/sweepstakes entries.

Information from connected services (only if you connect them)

We may combine personal information we receive from you with personal information that we obtain when you authorize a connection:

  • Apple (Sign in with Apple). Identity token, Apple user identifier, and, when Apple provides them, your name and email. We use native ID-token authentication through our auth provider; we do not operate a separate Apple web OAuth login.
  • Apple (HealthKit). On your device, the Service may read HealthKit data you allow, currently used for daily steps, active energy, sleep (hours and episode timestamps used for the Deprivation graph), and gym / strength-session counts. Raw HealthKit samples remain on the device. If you are signed in with Apple and use Real data mode, the Service may upload daily aggregates only (not raw samples) to your account so they can appear in the Service. Mock data, the isolated first-time preview, anonymous/device sessions, and Recovery / readiness values are not uploaded. Recovery has no real provider in the current product.
  • Plaid (bank or card). After you complete Plaid Hosted Link in the in-app browser, our servers receive connection credentials and transaction history needed to draw spending graphs (for example merchant description, amount, dates, and Plaid categories). LifeMerge does not collect your bank password, PIN, or card number. Access tokens are stored on the server, encrypted. You may disconnect, which revokes the Plaid Item and deletes imported bank records for that connection.
  • GitHub. After you authorize GitHub in the system web-authentication session, our servers receive an OAuth token (encrypted at rest) and your contribution calendar (daily counts, and on selected days a breakdown of commits, pull requests opened, reviews, issues opened, and restricted/private contribution counts). The current requested scope is read:user only. We do not request repo or user:email access and do not download repository names, source code, issue titles, or pull-request titles.

Anonymous and device sessions

The Service can create an anonymous / device session through our auth provider so the app can run before Sign in with Apple. Anonymous sessions are not treated as a full customer account for production bank linking or Health cloud sync. When you sign in with Apple, a device session that already has stored data may be linked in place so that data stays with the same account; an empty device session may sign in to a new or existing Apple account. We do not silently merge two populated accounts.

Automatic data collection

We, and our service providers, may automatically log information about your device and your use of the Service, such as:

  1. Device and session data, such as operating system type, app version, and identifiers needed to maintain your auth session on the device (sessions are stored in the device Keychain).
  2. Product analytics events via PostHog, by design limited to event names and coarse status or reason codes (for example that Sign in with Apple started or was canceled; that HealthKit, GitHub, or bank connect succeeded or failed; that a graph was interacted with). Identify, when used, is the internal account UUID after Apple sign-in. Reset runs on sign-out.
  3. Online activity data in that limited analytics sense (screens or event names we choose to record). We disable PostHog session replay, automatic screen capture, application-lifecycle capture, surveys, element interaction capture, and automatic error tracking in the current implementation.

We do not collect precise GPS / device-location data. Graphs use a fixed reporting calendar (currently Gregorian, Monday-first, America/Los_Angeles) as a display and aggregation convention, not as a location trace.

Analytics exclusions (by current product design). PostHog events are not intended to include HealthKit values, bank amounts, merchant names, transaction IDs, Apple name or email, access tokens, contribution-calendar counts, or session replay. A particular build may have analytics disabled if the configuration file is absent.
